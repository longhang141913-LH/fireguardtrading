Fire Extinguisher Refilling Plants in the Middle East & North Africa: 19 Plants in 9 Countries, Mapped

If you ship empty or partially discharged cylinders into the Gulf, your real partner is not the buyer — it is whoever refills, stamps and certifies those cylinders at the other end. This page maps the plants that actually hold that capability, with the addresses and websites you can verify yourself, and it is honest about the one thing the industry will not tell you: almost nobody publishes how much they refill.

⚠️ How this list was built — read before you use it

Every company below was checked against its own website, its published contact page, or a business registry entry in October–November 2026. We did not invent or estimate a single figure.

  • "n/d" means not disclosed. It does not mean zero. Only six of the nineteen companies publish any headcount at all.
  • Figures marked * are directory estimates, not company statements, and should be treated as a range, not a fact.
  • No plant publishes refill volume — cylinders filled per year, per month, or per shift. This is the single biggest gap in the public record and we say so rather than filling it.
  • Nothing here is paid placement. These are competitors and peers, listed because an importer needs to know who is out there.
  • Prices, availability and certification status change. Call before you contract; a website is not a capability audit.

1. What actually counts as a "refilling plant"

The phrase is used loosely in this region. Buying agents will happily call a trading office with a single filling rig a "factory". For an importer of cylinders, three very different things get sold under the same name, and it is worth knowing which one you are talking to.

The 19 plants, split by what they can actually do in-house

Classification based on each company's own description of its facility. Nine own a manufacturing line; four run a refilling workshop; six are contractors whose refilling depends on a partner or a small in-house rig.

9Manufacturer with a filling line

Makes the cylinder body or assembles the unit, then fills, tests and stamps it in the same plant. Can issue its own test certificates.

NAFFCO, FIREX, SFFECO, Amanco, Meteory, Albtran, MORX, Stop Fire, SCHIP

4Dedicated refilling workshop

Buys cylinders in, or takes yours, and refills, hydro-tests and re-labels. Often faster on small batches than a factory.

Aromax, A-TEAM, Majan National, Asayab

6Contractor / systems house

Designs and installs fire systems; refilling is a service line, sometimes subcontracted. Good for AMC contracts, weaker as a filling partner.

Q Fire, Advance Technical Services, Peninsula Engineering, Alpha, NF Safety, Desautel Algérie

For anyone landing cylinders from China, the first tier is the one to prioritise: only a plant that fills on its own line can give you a test certificate with its own name on it, and that certificate is what your buyer's Civil Defence inspector will ask for.

6. Reading the list by region

The Gulf: big factories, thin middle

The UAE, Saudi Arabia and Oman are dominated by a small number of vertically integrated manufacturers — NAFFCO, FIREX, SFFECO, Amanco — each of which makes, fills, tests and certifies in the same plant. Below them there is very little in the 100-to-300-person band. You either work with a national champion or with a 15-person workshop; the comfortable mid-size refiller barely exists. That gap is why so many Gulf importers end up going straight to a manufacturer even for a 200-unit order.

Qatar and Bahrain: contractors that refill

Both markets are small enough that refilling is bundled into maintenance contracts rather than sold as a standalone service. Q Fire, Advance Technical Services, Alpha and NF Safety all present themselves as systems contractors first. If you need cylinders filled, you will be buying an AMC, not a filling slot.

Egypt: the deepest manufacturing bench

Egypt is the only market on this list with three genuine manufacturers clustered in one industrial zone — the 10th of Ramadan City, east of Cairo. Meteory has been there since 1986, Albtran since 2005, MORX since 2019. Labour cost is the reason: Egyptian extinguisher manufacturing competes with China on assembly cost in a way that Gulf plants cannot. For importers weighing a local partner against a Chinese supplier, Egypt is the market where the comparison is genuinely close.

Morocco: old, export-minded, French-influenced

Three of the four oldest companies on this list are Moroccan. SCHIP dates to 1970, Stop Fire to 1982, and both operate in Casablanca's industrial belt. Moroccan plants are also the most export-oriented in North Africa — Stop Fire ships roughly 30% of output abroad, mostly south into Africa. If you are looking for a refilling partner to serve West African buyers, Casablanca is a more logical base than Dubai.

Where the record goes quiet

Algeria, Tunisia and Libya have real refilling capacity but almost no verifiable public footprint. Desautel Algérie is the only entry we could confirm to a stable corporate address. This is not a claim that nothing exists there — it is a statement that a foreign importer cannot build a shortlist for those markets from public sources, and will need a local agent.

7. Four conclusions an importer can actually use

  1. Refilling is a manufacturer's side business, not a standalone industry. Nine of nineteen plants exist primarily to make things; filling is a service line attached to production. Plan around the factory, not around a "refilling company".
  2. The industry's centre of gravity is 1980s Casablanca and Cairo, not 2010s Dubai. If longevity signals reliability, the North African plants have a 20-year head start on most Gulf names.
  3. Headcount is not a proxy for capability here. The two most specific service numbers we found came from a 54-person Egyptian factory and a 11–15-person Bahraini workshop — neither of which is large.
  4. Nobody will tell you their volume. Since refill throughput is unpublished across the entire region, the only reliable way to judge a partner is a factory visit or a third-party inspection, not a brochure.

8. What this means if you are buying from China

The practical consequence is a two-step sourcing decision. First pick the destination plant — the one that will refill, stamp and certify — and only then size your container around what that plant can absorb. Getting this backwards is the most common expensive mistake we see: an importer fills a 40ft container with empty cylinders, then discovers the local partner can only test 60 a week.

A few rules that follow from the data above:

  • Ask for the test certificate before the price. A plant without its own hydrostatic line cannot issue one, and no amount of discount compensates.
  • Check the founding year against the certificate date. A 2019 plant quoting a 2005-style test document is telling you something.
  • Budget for the branch network, not the head office. SFFECO's four branches and Meteory's fifteen are the reason their units reach project sites; a single-address workshop will not.
  • Confirm Arabic labelling capability in writing. Every Gulf market requires it, and it is done at the filling plant, not at the port.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many fire extinguisher refilling plants are there in the Middle East?

There is no official count, and any single figure should be treated with suspicion. This page documents 19 plants across nine countries that publish a verifiable address and website. The true number is far higher — Gulf markets alone likely contain several hundred small KFFD-, QCDD- or Civil Defence-approved workshops — but the overwhelming majority have no web presence and cannot be verified remotely.

Why don't these companies publish how many cylinders they refill?

Because refilling is a low-margin service line attached to manufacturing or maintenance contracting, not a reported business segment. None of the companies here are listed on a stock exchange with segment disclosure requirements, so there is no regulatory pressure to publish throughput. In practice, refill volume is negotiated privately between plant and client.

Which country has the cheapest refilling in the region?

Egypt, on labour cost, followed by Morocco. Gulf plants carry higher overheads — free-zone rents, expatriate labour, certification fees — which feed into refilling prices. But refilling cost is rarely the deciding factor: freight and customs on the cylinders usually outweigh the per-unit filling difference, so the cheapest plant is often not the cheapest total.

Can a refilling workshop issue a hydrostatic test certificate?

Only if it owns and operates hydrostatic test equipment and is recognised by the local authority to do so. In the UAE that means MoIAT-registered facilities; in Saudi Arabia, SASO-recognised bodies; in Qatar, QCDD approval. A workshop that sends cylinders elsewhere for testing will return a certificate in someone else's name — which is a problem if your buyer's inspector checks the stamp against the supplier.

Is this list complete or ranked?

Neither. It is a verified sample, not a census, and the order is geographic rather than by size, revenue or capability. No company paid to appear. If you know of a plant that should be here, tell us and we will check it against its own published record before adding it.
